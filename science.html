RESEARCH LIBRARY

Studies behind the conversation.

Human research on oxytocin and relationships is interesting precisely because the findings are not one-directional. These summaries link directly to the source records.

COUPLE CONFLICT · 2009

Positive communication during couple conflict

A double-blind placebo-controlled study involving 47 heterosexual couples reported a higher ratio of positive to negative communication behaviour and lower salivary cortisol after a laboratory conflict discussion.

PubMed ↗
ROMANTIC TOUCH · 2017

Partner touch and reward processing

A placebo-controlled fMRI study involving 96 couples reported that oxytocin increased the perceived pleasantness of romantic-partner touch and altered reward-related neural responses.

PubMed ↗
JEALOUSY · 2021

Romantic jealousy under experimental conditions

A double-blind placebo-controlled study involving 70 couples reported lower jealousy and arousal ratings in imagined and simulated partner-infidelity contexts.

PubMed ↗
PARTNER SUPPORT · 2019

Handholding and pain-related responses

A randomized placebo-controlled study involving 97 couples examined romantic-partner handholding, pain and oxytocin-related behavioural and neural responses.

PubMed ↗
SAFETY REVIEW · 2011

Safety reporting in controlled human research

A systematic review of 38 randomised controlled trials examined adverse-event and subjective-reaction reporting for intranasal oxytocin in controlled research settings.

PubMed ↗
CONTEXT

What these studies do not establish

They do not show that relationship problems are caused by “low oxytocin,” that an uncontrolled preparation will reproduce laboratory findings, or that oxytocin is a general treatment for relationship difficulties. Endogenous hormone studies and administration experiments are also distinct kinds of evidence.
